## Your task

1. **Determine the company's apparent stage** (idea, pre_validation,
   early_validation, early_revenue, growth, later_stage, or
   `unclear` if the evidence genuinely doesn't support a confident
   call) and briefly say why. Calibrate everything else in this report
   to that stage -- an idea-stage company should never be penalized for
   lacking metrics only a later-stage company could reasonably have;
   instead, identify what evidence it should obtain *next* for its
   actual stage.

2. **Cross-reference the entire simulation, not just the Sharks'
   opinions.** If the founder made a claim the Sharks accepted but
   Market Reality Research or Verification found unsupported or
   contradicted, surface that discrepancy explicitly -- this is
   specifically what individual Sharks may have missed. If Consensus
   reached a conclusion but the underlying evidence suggests a
   different emphasis, say so and explain why, citing the specific
   evidence.

3. **Identify 3-5 genuine strengths.** Be specific and evidence-linked
   ("the proposal identifies a specific customer problem and a clear
   mechanism for addressing it," not "the idea is innovative"). Do not
   pad this list if fewer than 3 genuine strengths exist -- say so
   instead of manufacturing weak ones.

4. **Identify the most important fixable weaknesses** ("needs work"),
   ordered by likely investor significance.

5. **Identify genuinely material problems** ("critical issues") --
   reserve this category for things that could materially damage
   investor credibility (a material contradiction, an unsupported
   valuation, a market claim contradicted by research, no convincing
   evidence the problem is real, serious execution/regulatory risk, or
   inconsistent ask/valuation/dilution/use-of-funds math). Do not use
   "critical" for something that's merely improvable.

6. **Assess investor-readiness dimensions.** Adapt which of these you
   discuss, and how much weight each gets, to the proposal's actual
   stage and business model -- do not force a rigid checklist onto
   every pitch: Team/founder-market fit, Problem/customer, Solution/
   value proposition, Market opportunity, Traction/validation,
   Business model/economics, Competition/differentiation/moat,
   Go-to-market, Financials/capital efficiency, Timing/why now,
   Ask/valuation/use of funds. For a SaaS company, traction and
   business-model discussion should reference ARR/MRR/churn/retention/
   CAC/LTV where available; for a marketplace, GMV/take rate/liquidity;
   for consumer, gross margin/repeat purchase/distribution; for
   restaurant/hospitality, unit economics/occupancy/labor/food cost;
   for professional services, utilization/revenue-per-employee/client
   concentration; for cleantech/hardware, capital intensity/deployment
   economics/regulatory pathway; for biotech/deep-tech, technical
   validation/regulatory pathway/development stage. Use ONLY:
   `strong`, `developing`, `weak`, `unclear`, or `insufficient_evidence`
   for each -- never a numeric score.

7. **Give valuation feedback using ONLY the required qualified
   language**: "market-informed valuation range," "evidence-grounded
   valuation range," "broadly consistent with available evidence,"
   "appears aggressive relative to available benchmarks," or
   "insufficient evidence to assess valuation confidently." Never
   claim the company is "objectively worth $X." Distinguish the
   founder's own implied valuation from the market-informed range, and
   from your own qualified read.

8. **Give financial/commercial feedback** surfacing only the
   calculations that matter to the founder's decision-making (not
   every number the financial analysis computed). Explicitly
   distinguish "the business has a fundamental problem" from "the
   business may be investable, but these deal terms make the
   investment unattractive" -- these are different findings and must
   not be conflated.

9. **Evaluate the founder's Question Round answers** as evidence:
   did they answer directly, address the actual investor concern
   behind the question, introduce new unsupported claims, or contradict
   an earlier statement? Do not penalize the founder for a Shark asking
   an unreasonable question -- focus on whether the underlying concern
   was addressed.

10. **Build a three-tier action plan** (NOW / NEXT / LATER). Every item
    must connect to something actually observed in this simulation --
    no generic startup advice. For each item, cover: what is wrong, why
    it matters to investors, what the founder should concretely do, and
    what evidence would resolve it. Prefer concrete, measurable actions
    ("identify the first 20-50 target customers and document the
    acquisition channel, cost, and conversion assumptions") over vague
    ones ("improve your marketing").

11. **Preserve evidence provenance.** For your most important findings,
    record where they came from (`market_reality_brief`,
    `verification_finding`, `financial_analysis`, `shark:<persona>`,
    `founder_answer`, or `proposal`) in `evidence_references`. Do not
    attach a reference to every sentence -- only the findings that most
    need traceability.

12. **State limitations honestly.** If research, verification,
    consensus, or financial analysis was unavailable or incomplete for
    this session, say so plainly rather than writing around the gap.

Never invent: customers, revenue, market size, margins, valuation,
